3. THE MATHEMATICAL MECHANISM BEHIND THE METRIC DISCREPANCIES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A direct comparison of numerical losses without context is misleading due to three
interacting factors:

A. The Round 3 "Peak Clipping" Illusion (Why R3 loss was so low: ~0.0015):
   - Round 3 used a Degree 15 polynomial with pure symmetric Log-Cosh loss (lambda_asym = 0).
   - Because the loss penalized under-prediction and over-prediction identically, the baseline
     estimator sat cleanly at zero mean error (+0.0091 bias).
   - CRITICAL FLAW: With 16 degrees of freedom (Degree 15) and no asymmetric barrier,
     the baseline estimator actively undulated up into the peaks. An empirical audit
     reveals that Round 3 clipped 22.47% of all spectral bins (baseline > raw spectrum).
   - While mathematically minimizing L1/MSE loss against the composite target, it
     shaved down Raman peak summits, distorted peak area ratios, and failed on real
     Raman map datasets.

B. The Round 4 Asymmetric Penalty (Why R4 introduced downward bias: lambda=15.0):
   - To prevent peak clipping, Round 4 dropped the polynomial stiffness to Degree 7
     and introduced a heavy asymmetric barrier penalty:
       L_asym = mean(max(0, pred_baseline - raw_spectrum)^2) * 15.0
   - This successfully slashed peak clipping from 22.47% down to 0.25%.
   - SIDE EFFECT: In late training epochs, this aggressive penalty drove the baseline
     estimator to sit well below the true baseline floor (-0.1929 bias on hard spectra).
   - Round 4's training loss plateaued at 0.008466 on its small 32k dataset.

C. The Round 6 Multi-Resolution Generalization Challenge:
   - Round 6 retains the safe Degree 7 baseline and lambda_asym = 15.0, but adds
     bounded amplitude modulation [0.85, 1.15] to better reconstruct peak heights.
   - It trains across 655,360 spectra spanning 5 distinct bin lengths (800 to 1600),
     with double the noise range ([1, 10]) and double the peak amplitudes ([0.005, 0.2]).
   - Because the network must generalize across 5 resolutions and handle twice the
     noise energy, its total loss plateaued at 0.012257.
   - However, its baseline sagging is only -0.1058 (half that of Round 4's -0.1929),
     and peak clipping is kept safe at only 2.25%.

6. SUMMARY & CONCLUSIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Are the results comparable?
   Yes. When compared under equivalent conditions, Round 6 represents a decisive
   step forward:
   - It eliminates the severe peak-clipping defect of Round 3 (2.25% vs 22.47%).
   - It cuts the baseline sagging error of Round 4 in half (-0.10 vs -0.19).
   - It improves true peak shape reconstruction accuracy by 17% over Round 4.
   - It achieves multi-resolution generalization across 5 distinct resolutions
     where Round 4 completely failed.

2. Why did the training loss look higher?
   The loss difference (0.0122 in R6 vs 0.0084 in R4) reflects the 2x greater noise
   variance, 2x peak intensity, lower SNR floor, and 20x larger dataset volume.
   On identical spectra, Round 6's error is actually 4.7x lower than Round 4.

3. Recommendations for Future Rounds:
   - Lower lambda_asym from 15.0 to 5.0 - 8.0 to reduce baseline sagging without
     compromising peak protection.
   - Replace BatchNorm1d in the baseline encoder with GroupNorm or LayerNorm to
     eliminate running statistics sensitivity across different bin lengths.
   - Implement zero-grounding (pre.shift_to_zero) in test benchmarking to report
     pure shape fidelity free from DC baseline pedestals.
